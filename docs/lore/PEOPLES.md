# Vaelora: the peoples

Part of the lore bible (`LORE.md`). You said "you decide" for every people, so nearly all of
this is ***(suggested)***; what was already decided is marked **decided**. **🔒** is what
almost nobody in the world knows.

Every people has its dignified and its twisted sorts. There's tension, as everywhere, but for
the most part anyone can be anything. Where each people stood at the Arrival is what defines
them.

---

## Humans

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Everywhere, but most of all the heartlands and the Riverlands of Orveth: farms, river towns, walled cities |
| **Who leads** | The **Ilunar Empire**: Empress Corvina in Ilunhall, governors in the provinces, councils in the towns |
| **What they value** | Land, family, and getting ahead. A human will try anything once, which is why most of the Collegium's students are human |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"The Gift."** The lights came down to teach the worthy, and humanity was worthy. (The official version, taught everywhere.) |
| **What others say** | Greedy, short-lived, everywhere, and ruling all of it |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Stubborn builders who hold the line / climbers who'll sell anyone to rise |
| **The dead** | Buried in barrows with grave-goods, the old way (the Old Barrow is human). In cities, burned since the Hollowing scares |
| **Wisps** | Good luck at a farmhouse window; pests in town; children are told they drink your breath |
| **Names** | Celtic/Gaelic in the valleys (Maren, Eilidh), Norse/Germanic in the north (Brannoc, Sigrun), Latin/Imperial in the cities and the court (Corvina, Vessa) |

🔒 Human kings led the pact. The Empire was founded on the Hunt, and its first Emperor was a
hunter.

## Elves

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **Ithmarren**, the forest city in the giant trees, and elven quarters in the great cities |
| **Who leads** | The **Great Houses**, old families with long memories, meeting as the Moot of Houses. Lineage matters more than anything |
| **What they value** | Lineage, mastery, and beauty. They live about four hundred years and plan accordingly |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"The Kindling."** The lights kindled the elves into what they are: long-lived, brilliant, closest to magic |
| **What others say** | Arrogant and brilliant, and half of them a sip away from corruption. "Never leave an elf alone with a wisp" |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Scholars and healers, the Collegium's finest / essence addicts who'd drain anything that glows |
| **The dead** | Sung into the roots of the great trees |
| **Wisps** | Sacred in public. In private, some houses run the essence trade |
| **Names** | Invented and flowing (Ithariel, Sennaveth) |

**Decided:** elves are the people most changed by the Arrival, the most prone to corruption,
and 🔒 **they drank the most Vael**, which is why they live so long.

🔒 "The Kindling" is true, in the worst way: they're what drinking the Vael made them.

## Orcs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **The Iron Moors**: clan-holds in the high north-east, forge-towns, mountain passes. Orc legions are the Empire's steadiest troops |
| **Who leads** | **Oath-chiefs**, chosen by their deeds and answerable to a clan moot. Oaths are sworn on iron |
| **What they value** | Oaths, work, and memory |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"The Taking."** Their old songs say something was taken, and that orcs stood by while it happened, and some helped. They swore never again. That's why they distrust easy magic |
| **What others say** | Brutes (they aren't), stubborn, and holders of grudges older than the Empire |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Oath-keepers / oath-breakers who sell their axes to whoever pays, the Vaerd included |
| **The dead** | Burned on a forge-pyre, and the ashes worked into iron, so nothing can drink from them |
| **Wisps** | Left alone. Children are taught never to touch one. An orc who drinks essence is an oath-breaker |
| **Mages** | Rare and formidable. *(suggested)* Orc mages swear an oath for each spell they learn |
| **Names** | Hard and guttural (Grukka, Durzhak) |

**Decided:** 🔒 **orcs remember the killing**; their songs are the nearest thing to the truth
that anyone sings openly, and nobody listens.

## The Passengers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | No homeland. A quarter in every city (the **Lanes**), river-boat clans on the Riverlands, and families who've lived in one town for three hundred years and are still called newcomers |
| **Who leads** | Their **elders**, who keep the old songs. 🔒 The oldest songs are kept by a hidden circle, **the Choir** |
| **What they value** | Memory, belonging, and hiding well |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"We came with them."** They say it quietly. The songs say they came down with the lights and failed them, and the songs don't say how |
| **What others say** | **They caused the Hollowing** (decided: a myth). Shifty, cursed, and the first to be blamed when an outbreak comes |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Song-keepers who remember what everyone else forgot / informers and essence-dealers who sold their own memory for coin |
| **The dead** | **Given to rivers**, "so the water carries us after them" |
| **Wisps** | Grief. A wisp at your window is someone you owe |
| **Looks** | Tall and thin, with eyes a shade too large and too pale |
| **Names** | Open (round four) |

**Decided:** they came with the Vael, and they're quietly discriminated against because of a
myth that they caused the Hollowing. 🔒 The irony is that the only people who remember the Vael
alive are blamed for what everyone else did.

## The Ashen

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **The Grieving Fields**: quiet grey villages on the old battlefields, and near the twelve sites |
| **Who leads** | **The Listeners**: whoever hears the wisps most clearly. It isn't always the oldest, and it isn't a gift anyone wants |
| **What they value** | Silence, endurance, and remembering what others forgot |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"We were emptied, and something stayed."** They know their ancestors were hollowed. They don't know by what |
| **What others say** | Cursed, unlucky and uncanny. Elves say they're no kin of theirs |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Listeners, guides and wisp-mourners / wisp-finders for hire who sell the lights to drinkers (decided: they're hired as wisp-finders, and hate it) |
| **The dead** | Laid out in the open on the battlefields, "so the lights can see us" |
| **Wisps** | Kin. They feel them faintly, and feel them hurt |
| **Looks** | Grey-skinned, quiet, pale-eyed |
| **Names** | Open (round four) |

**The tension with elves (decided):** 🔒 most of the hunters who hollowed were elves, because
elves drank the most. The Ashen descend from them. The great houses cast their hollowed
ancestors out, and call the Ashen cursed rather than admit the kinship. The **Ashen Rising**
(340–360 AA) was the war that followed. Neither side has forgiven the peace.

## Small folk

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **The Hearthlands**, rolling hills of inns, orchards and market towns. Every road in the Empire also has a small-folk inn on it (decided: innkeepers, couriers and brokers) |
| **Who leads** | Family councils and **very** long meetings, the **Moot** |
| **What they value** | Comfort, fairness, a good deal, and discretion |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"We said no."** The small folk turned down the lights' magic, and that's why "small folk don't do spells". 🔒 It's a lie, and only Aeviro knows it: they said yes, then swore off magic out of shame |
| **What others say** | Harmless, nosy, honest, and lucky. Everyone trusts them as go-betweens, because they don't do spells |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Peacemakers and honest brokers / swindlers and fences |
| **The dead** | Buried under the hearthstone or in the garden, with a meal |
| **Wisps** | Shooed away: "not our business" |
| **Names** | Open (round four); Aeviro is one |

## Deepfolk

| | |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **Karrowdeep**, the city in the mountain, and mining halls under hills across Orveth (decided: guild-law mining halls) |
| **Who leads** | **The Guilds** under **guild-law**: contracts carved in stone, and a Table of Guildmasters |
| **What they value** | Craft, contracts, and the depth of a seam |
| **Their story of the Arrival** | **"The sky-folk left us treasure."** The residue is an inheritance, and they're its rightful miners |
| **What others say** | Greedy, grim, rich, and blind to anything above ground |
| **Dignified / twisted** | Master crafters and rune-smiths / strip-miners who'd dig the twelve wells dry for one more season (decided: frightened as the stones give less) |
| **The dead** | Sealed in the rock, deep, "where nothing can reach them" |
| **Wisps** | Rarely seen underground. Essence is a commodity with a price per dram |
| **Names** | Open (round four) |

🔒 The deepest seams whisper. Some Deepfolk miners are starting to suspect what the residue is.
