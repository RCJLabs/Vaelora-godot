# Vaelora: the game's story

Part of the lore bible (`LORE.md`). **Plain text** is what you decided (round four, 24 October
2026); ***(suggested)*** is what I filled in; **🔒** is what the player learns late or not at all.

---

## The full game's shape

- **Skyrim-sized, across several continents.** 25 to 40 main quests.
- **Acts, with an open middle.**
- **The Greywater Vale (the demo) becomes the first act.**
- *(suggested)* The acts:
  1. **The Greywater Vale** (the demo, extended). Small wrong things, the Mercy, The First,
     the letter and the map of twelve marks. Ends on the cliffhanger the demo already has.
  2. **The open middle.** The twelve sites, in any order, across Orveth, Sundra and the
     Scatter. Each site is a quest. The Mercy recur, some of them sympathetic. The Lantern Order
     hunts you, harder the more corrupted you are. The guilds and colleges live here too. The
     Choir's verses turn up one at a time.
  3. **The truth.** Past the middle, closer to the end: the truth scene (below).
  4. **The Pale Gate.** The sea journey north, Aeviro's hut on the wandering island, and the
     choice.

## The player

- **A nobody who becomes a legend by what they do**, with a stake from their background (the
  six openings). Never a chosen one.
- **Corruption changes the endings, and opens and closes whole paths**: a hollowed player can
  join things others can't, and the Lantern Order won't have them.
- **The player can become the vessel instead of Aeviro, by choice, whatever their corruption.**

## The threads of the main quest

- **Each of the twelve sites is a quest.**
- **The Mercy as recurring enemies, and some of them sympathetic.**
- **The Lantern Order hunting you.**
- **Magister Vessa** is **redeemable: she can turn and help you, and the player decides.**
  *(suggested)* Spare her and help her toward the cure she was promised, and she becomes your way
  into the Mercy. Hand her to the Lantern Order and she burns.

## The truth scene

- **The artifact** you're sent deep to recover is **the founders' confession: the only record
  nobody burned.** *(suggested)* Signed by the pact's kings and the Collegium's founders: what
  they did, and how.
- **Aeviro took it before you got there.** In its place waits something terrible. You kill it,
  it releases wisps, and before you can choose what to do with them, "A." walks in and absorbs
  them all. "A." is the small-folk card player from the start.
- **When:** past the middle of the game, closer to the end than the beginning.
- **How the truth comes out: both ways.** Big scenes nobody can miss, like this one, and pieces
  for the curious: the Choir's verses, books, the wisps' voices at Hollowing, and Maren's gran's
  card player.

## The endings

**Three endings, with slides for each faction and place, plus a fourth, hidden one.**
*(suggested)* Each ending's slides also change with your corruption and every essence choice
you made.

1. **Aeviro's way:** you help Aeviro finish.
   - Aeviro takes the gathered Vael into themselves, crosses the Pale Gate, and dies emptied as
     the Vael leave.
   - **Or you go in Aeviro's place.** That's the **only ending where anyone goes up to the
     Wanderer**: you carry the Vael up to their ship, and don't come back.
2. **Stop them.** Aeviro dies or is stopped, and the gathering ends. The Vael stay scattered.
3. **Take it.** You take the gathered Vael for yourself, and all that power. The Dimming ends,
   because the magic now runs through you.
4. ***(suggested)* The hidden ending: Lantern Night.**
   - **What it needs:**
     - the founders' confession, taken back from Aeviro;
     - every verse of the Choir's song;
     - enough trust with the Empire, the Collegium and the Passengers to be heard.
   - **What happens:** on Lanternfast in Ilunhall, you read the confession aloud in the Hall of
     Spears. The Choir sings the song in full for the first time in nine hundred years. For one
     night, every lantern in the Empire is lit for the Passengers' reason: "so the lost lights
     can find their way".
   - The Vael don't need a vessel. They gather themselves, from every window, and leave
     together.
   - Aeviro, unburdened, lets go of the Vael inside them, and dies of old age at last. That's
     what they wanted, without the cruelty.
   - It's the only ending where nobody has to be the price. It rewards the curious, not the
     strong.

### What happens to the wisps *(suggested)*

| Ending | The wisps |
|---|---|
| Aeviro's way | The gathered leave alive, on the Wanderer, and the green moon leaves the sky. Released ones are at rest. The ones you absorbed leave you too: your corruption, and its power, go with them |
| You, as the vessel | The same, but the slides are about the people you left behind |
| Stop them | They stay in the world, scattered and in pain. The ending slides show the slow work, over generations, of releasing them one by one. The Wanderer hangs empty in the sky forever |
| Take it | They're inside you, and you can hear every one of them. Magic floods back into the world. The last slide is the player, alone, listening |
| Lantern Night | Every wisp in the world rises at once toward the lanterns and leaves together. Lanternfast becomes a remembrance. The Wanderer leaves the sky |

## Companions

**The wisp companion, Mirela, Brannoc, Zora, Dunya, an Ashen listener, and a Passenger
song-keeper from the Choir.** **Romance with some of them.**

- **The wisp companion** is **a piece of someone Aeviro needs, and Aeviro wants it back**, and
  **its fate depends on your essence choices**. *(suggested)* It's part of the Vael that Aeviro
  loved, the one Aeviro killed first. Release enough wisps and it trusts you enough to choose
  for itself. Absorb too many and it's afraid of you.
- *(suggested)* **The Ashen listener: Still.** A wisp-finder who hates the work, hears the
  wisps, and hears the ones inside you.
- *(suggested)* **The Passenger song-keeper: Saelie of the Low Lane.** Young for the Choir,
  carrying verses she's afraid to sing.
- *(suggested)* **Romance:** Mirela, Brannoc, Zora, Still and Saelie. Not Dunya; her story is
  hollowing. Not the wisp.

## The demo now (decided: built)

- Release an essence at the stone circle's crystal (rest), as well as swallowing it (power).
  Releasing gives **Wisp's Grace**: the wisps let you be that night.
- Night wisps notice a corrupted player from further away, and more of them come out.
- Maren mentions the old small-folk card player.
- The schools are renamed **Ruin, Mending and Shaping**.
- Ostap sings a verse of "Hallam and the Hundred Lights".
- The cult's notes and letter name **the Mercy**.
- **The cliffhanger stays as it is:** the letter and the map of twelve marks.

## Names for the four new peoples *(suggested)*

| People | Style | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **The Passengers** | Soft, sung names; a family takes its lane or boat as a surname | Saelie, Ilyesse, Orrin, Tavio; Saelie of the Low Lane |
| **The Ashen** | One short name, often a plain word | Still, Wren, Hollin, Ember, Moss |
| **Small folk** | Homely given names, and surnames from hearth and orchard | Tamsin Kettlewick, Nob Ashby, Hettie Marlow. Aeviro's name is old-tongue, older than any of these |
| **Deepfolk** | Given name and a craft-name earned in a guild | Karra Seamwright, Dorn Veinhand, Bruna Deepseal |

## Which new people becomes playable first *(suggested)*

**The Passengers**, then the Ashen, Deepfolk and small folk:
- The Passengers are the cheapest to build (the human body with a different face and
  proportions), and the richest in story (blamed for the Hollowing; the Choir).
- The Ashen are almost as cheap (a skin shader).
- Deepfolk need a broader, shorter body.
- Small folk need a child-scale body, so they're the most expensive.

## Tone in play

- **Humour lives mostly in side quests, guilds and characters, and above all in Aeviro.**
- **Never shown on screen:** harm to children, animal cruelty, sexual violence, torture, and
  self-harm. (Gore is allowed.)
- **Voice:** text only, with a few voiced lines: the wisps, and Aeviro.
- **The last scene:** not decided yet.
