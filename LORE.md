## In one paragraph

**Vaelora** is a medieval world that took a different road. Long ago, living things from
another world arrived and brought magic, and strange creatures, with them. Instead of
inventing gunpowder, Vaelora's peoples learned magic. Anyone can learn it, few master it, and
used carelessly it corrupts the user. Now, in a river valley on the edge of an old forest,
the most magical beings are being found **drained of their life**. Someone is taking it,
and legends nobody believed say it's being used to build an army. You arrive as whoever you
chose to be: a soldier, a scholar, an outlaw. By the end of the demo you've followed the trail
far enough to see how big it is.

## Tone and pillars

| | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Feel most** | **Wonder** (strange ruins, impossible lights) and **power** (from nobody to legend) |
| **Hope vs. bleak** | Middle (3/5): the world has real danger and loss, but people and places worth saving |
| **Humour** | Dry and wry (4/5), like Fallout's: wit in characters and situations, never a joke at the world's expense |
| **Magic** | High (4/5): visible, spectacular at the top, learnable by anyone |
| **Strangeness** | High (4/5): familiar medieval ground with alien things in it |
| **Loves** | Skyrim and Fallout (freedom, sandbox, lore), Game of Thrones (dragons, characters, story), Project Zomboid (survival), Lord of the Rings (a vast world) |
| **Never** | A chosen-one prophecy; elves perfect and wise or orcs evil brutes; "ancient evil returns" as the only plot |

**The picture to aim for** (your answer to question 30): *walking over a hill crest at dusk,
hearing a curling scream, seeing a red-eyed monster look up at you, and then a powerful mage
flies past and runs right through it, killing it instantly.* That moment carries three things
the game should keep doing: dread in the wild, magic that is overwhelming at its peak, and
the promise that **you could become that mage**.

## The world

### The Arrival

Long ago *(suggested: about 900 years, in the early medieval era)*, **lifeforms from another
world** came to Vaelora. They brought **magic**, and **creatures** that had never existed
here. Everything changed after that.

- *(suggested)* Vaelorans call it **the Arrival**. Every people tells it differently (your
  answer to question 5): some say they themselves came with it, some say it came to them,
  some say it was a punishment.
- *(suggested)* The arrivals themselves are rarely seen and poorly understood. Scholars call
  them **the Vael** (the world's name is taken from theirs), others say "the Visitors". Are
  they still here? Hidden, gone, or living inside the magic itself? That's a mystery for the
  full game, not the demo.

### A different road

Magic arrived in medieval times, so instead of developing guns, people leaned into magic.
Vaelora looks medieval, with castles, walled river towns, longhouses at the forest edge and
guild halls. But its "technology" is magical: *(suggested)* rune-lit streets in rich towns,
enchanted locks, healing colleges instead of barber-surgeons, and no gunpowder at all.

### The sky

**Two moons, with tides of power.**
- *(suggested)* **Ilun**, the old moon, white, always there.
- *(suggested)* **The Wanderer**, a smaller green-tinged moon that appeared with the Arrival.
  When it's dark (a "new Wanderer"), old stones and buried relics wake. This ties to what's
  already in the game: the stone circle glows at the new moon, and wisps come out at night.
- *(suggested)* When both moons are full, magic surges: spells are stronger and corruption
  spreads faster. A future world event.

## Magic

- **Where it comes from:** the alien lifeforms brought it. With practice anyone can learn to
  use it, but few can master it.
- **What it costs:** using it **corruptly** corrupts you, physically (exhaustion, corruption,
  madness). *(suggested)* "Corrupt" practice means taking what isn't yours: drawing power from
  another living thing instead of from yourself and the world. That's exactly what the force
  behind the main threat is doing, at scale.
- **How people see mages:** both respected and feared. *(suggested)* Mages trained in the
  **colleges** are respected and licensed. An unlicensed mage is watched, and the Riverwatch
  garrison can arrest one. Hedge-mages in the forest don't care.
- *(suggested)* At the peak, masters can **fly**, as in your scene. Flight is far beyond the
  demo: something the player sees, and wants.

## Gods and belief

- **The gods are dead or absent, their followers still argue, and each race believes
  differently. Nobody knows the truth.**
- **Something answers prayers, but nobody knows what.** Magic is real; the gods are unknown.
- *(suggested)* The shrines in the demo (the Acolyte background's "old rites") honour gods
  who never answer in words, yet healing prayers really do work. Is it the gods, the magic,
  or the Vael? Another mystery for the full game.

## The peoples

**At least seven races in the full game.** Every race has its twisted and its dignified
sorts. There's racial tension, as everywhere, but for the most part anyone can be anything.

**In the demo:** Human, Elf and Orc are playable. Every new playable race needs models,
animation and voices, so the others appear in the lore, and maybe as rare NPCs, first.

| Race | *(suggested)* one line | Name style *(suggested)* |
|---|---|---|
| **Humans** | The most numerous; river-traders, farmers and garrison soldiers, practical and stubborn. Most of the colleges' students are human, because they'll try anything | Celtic/Gaelic in the valley (Maren, Eilidh), Norse/Germanic in the borderland (Brannoc, Sigrun) |
| **Elves** | Long-lived, and the people most changed by the Arrival. Some say it made them. Brilliant mages, but also the most prone to corruption. Proud families with old debts | Invented and flowing (Ithariel, Sennaveth) |

*(suggested)* Two more name styles have a place: **Latin/Imperial** for the colleges and old
institutions (titles, spell names, the law), and **Slavic** for the forest folk and outlaws.

## The demo region

**A temperate river valley in autumn, at the edge of a wild borderland forest**: farmland
and colour by the river, deep old woods beyond it. *(suggested)* Name: **the Greywater Vale**,
with **the Thornwood** to the north.

What's there (already in the game, kept):
- **Riverwatch**: the garrison town on the river (off-screen in the test level).
- **The north road** and **Maren's waystation**.
- **The stone circle** with its waking crystal, and **the Waking Stones** quest.
- **The Greyfang pack** in Greyfang Hollow.
- **The bandit camp**.
- **The Old Barrow** and its **Stone Guardian**.
- **Wisps** at the stones by night. *(suggested)* They're small magical beings, which makes
  them the first victims of the drain (see below).

## Factions (reputation only in the demo; joining comes in the full game)

| Faction | Where | Stance |
|---|---|---|
| **Riverwatch Garrison** | The town | The law, stretched thin. Licenses mages and hangs bandits |
| **The outlaws** *(suggested name: the Thornwood Free)* | The forest | Say the law is the real problem: taxes, mage licences, a garrison that never comes when farms burn. Some of them are just thieves, some have a point |

The current bandit camp becomes the rough edge of the outlaws: the ones who stopped having a point.

## The main threat: the Draining *(name suggested)*

An **unknown force is stealing the lifeforce of the most magical beings** and building an
unknown army. There are legends, but no one thought it could be real.

To keep clear of "ancient evil returns" and of a chosen one, this is my suggestion:
- *(suggested)* **It's people, and it's new.** Behind the legend is a circle of corrupt mages
  who learned to take life from magical beings. That's exactly the corruption the colleges
  warn about, done on purpose. The legend is old; the people living it out are not.
- *(suggested)* **What the player sees first:** wisps fading at the stones, a Greyfang wolf
  found hollow and grey, the stone circle waking early. Small wrong things.
- *(suggested)* **Monsters stay rare** (your answer to question 24). Most fights are people:
  bandits, outlaws, cultists. Their "army" is mostly hired swords so far, plus a few
  creatures stuffed with stolen life.
- **The demo ends with one creature boss seeping with magic** (your answer to question 23).
  *(suggested)* It's the first of the army's creatures: a beast swollen and unstable with
  stolen life, half-finished. Killing it shows how much life they've already taken, and
  where it was sent. That's the cliffhanger.
- **One ending, but how you got there changes what people say about you**, and the demo ends
  on that cliffhanger. Riverwatch and the outlaws remember who helped them.

## Death: you wake up wounded

Death means **waking up wounded**: sometimes captured by enemies, sometimes found by allies.
The point is variety.

*(suggested)* What that could look like:
- **Found:** you wake at the waystation or a camp, with a lasting injury (lower maximum
  health for a day) and a cost (gold, or a lost item).
- **Captured:** if bandits or outlaws beat you, you wake in their camp without your gear, and
  escaping is a short sequence: sneak, fight, or talk.
- **Left for dead:** in the wild, you wake where you fell, hours later, hurt and cold.

*(suggested)* Build this for **Hardcore first**, with Normal keeping reload-your-save, then
consider it for both after playtesting. Capture needs its own escape scenes, so it's a real
chunk of work.
